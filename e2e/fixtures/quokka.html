Field Notes: The Quokka of Rottnest Island

The quokka is a small marsupial about the size of a domestic cat. It lives mostly on Rottnest Island off the coast of Western Australia, where the absence of foxes and cats has allowed a large population to thrive. Visitors know it for its rounded face and an expression that looks remarkably like a smile.

Quokkas are mainly nocturnal and spend the hottest part of the day resting in dense vegetation. They browse on grasses, leaves and shrubs, and can survive long periods with little fresh water by getting moisture from the plants they eat. Females usually raise a single joey each year in a pouch.

Because the animals are so approachable, the island has strict rules: feeding or touching them is prohibited. Human food makes them ill, and handling can injure them. Researchers track the population each year to monitor the effects of drought and tourism on this vulnerable species.
